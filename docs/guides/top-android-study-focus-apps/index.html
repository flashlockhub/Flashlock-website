Top Android apps for study and focus: five useful choices

By the FlashLock team · Published 16 November 2026 · Last reviewed 16 November 2026.

Quick answer: choose FlashLock for your own flashcards before selected apps, AnkiDroid for scheduled recall, Forest for timed focus, one sec for app-opening friction, or AppBlock for blocking routines. Start with the problem you need to solve, not the longest feature list.

Publisher disclosure: this is the FlashLock team’s editorial shortlist, with our own app listed first. It is not an independent ranking or a hands-on comparative test. We selected these apps by study use case and reviewed their official documentation on 16 November 2026; we did not measure learning outcomes or blocking strength.

1. FlashLock — put your cards before selected apps

FlashLock is an Android-only beta that puts a flashcard learning break before selected everyday apps. Use your own course material or import an Anki deck. It suits a specific moment: you reach for an app and want a short recall prompt before carrying on.

Keep the deck small enough to answer thoughtfully. This is not a substitute for a protected study session, worked problems or practical classes. There is no iPhone app. See the Android setup guide and Anki import instructions before deciding whether the workflow fits.

2. AnkiDroid — organise scheduled flashcard review

AnkiDroid is an Android client for the Anki spaced-repetition system. Its manual describes creating notes, downloading shared decks and scheduling reviews according to your responses. Choose it when managing a body of material and returning to due cards is your main task.

You still need to open the deck and do the work. Check shared cards against your syllabus rather than assuming they are accurate or complete. The official AnkiDroid manual explains study sessions, imports and synchronisation.

3. Forest — give a study block a clear timer

Forest offers a pomodoro-style focus timer with growing virtual trees and a Deep Focus blocking feature. It is available for Android and iOS. It suits a defined task such as reading a section, drafting a paragraph or attempting a problem set.

A finished timer records time, not understanding. Decide what you will produce before starting and check your work afterwards. Review Forest’s official features and download options for your platform; do not assume every feature behaves identically across devices.

4. one sec — interrupt automatic app openings

one sec offers app-opening interventions and scheduled blocking, with Android and iOS versions. Its website also describes a Lengo language-learning integration, so it is not merely a breathing timer. It may suit someone who wants to pause and reconsider an automatic app opening.

Features and integrations can differ by platform; we have not confirmed Lengo availability on Android. Check the official one sec feature and platform information rather than assuming every advertised integration is included on your phone.

5. AppBlock — set boundaries around distractions

AppBlock offers app and website blocking, scheduled profiles and Strict Mode to make blocks harder to bypass. It has Android and iOS versions, plus browser offerings. It suits planned boundaries around study rather than adding a recall question to an app opening.

Harder to bypass does not mean impossible to bypass. Check essential-app access and your settings before a long session. See AppBlock’s official product information for platform-specific details.

Choose one small experiment

  1. Name the obstacle: forgetting reviews, drifting during a task, or opening distractions automatically.
  2. Try one matching tool with a specific task and a manageable setting.
  3. After several sessions, review completed work and interruptions, not just streaks or time logged.

No app here guarantees better grades. Current prices and feature limits should be checked with each provider. For a repeatable routine, read how to stick with study habits.
